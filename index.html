What is mPOS?

💡 mPOS is a global digital accounting and settlement layer embedded inside existing banking systems. It enables near-instant cross-border settlement through coordinated local liquidity and dynamic international ledger balancing — without requiring traditional prefunding or real-time cross-border money movement.
🏦
Invisible to customersThe customer sees their bank. The bank uses mPOS underneath — just like Visa and Mastercard power card payments invisibly.
⚡
Near instant settlementValue moves through coordinated local payouts and digital ledger balancing — not physical cross-border transfer.
🌍
Any corridor, any directionNigeria–UK is the entry corridor. The same architecture scales to any corridor globally.
🔐
Compliance built inKYC, AML, tier limits, and random agent assignment are enforced at engine level — not added later.
🤝
Banks stay in controlmPOS works alongside banks — not around them. Banks keep their customer relationships. mPOS provides the settlement engine underneath.

How the Engine Works

💡 When a bank sends a transfer instruction to mPOS, the engine runs a sequence of checks automatically — in under a second. No human involvement. No manual routing. The engine decides everything.
1️⃣
Bank sends instructionThe bank authenticates its customer and sends a structured instruction to the mPOS engine via API.
2️⃣
Engine validatesAML screening, tier limits, FX rate lock, and transaction reference generation — all automatic.
3️⃣
Agents assigned randomlyEngine scans qualified agents on both sides and randomly assigns one each — neither party can choose the other.
4️⃣
Local payouts coordinatedSender agent receives fiat locally. Recipient agent pays recipient locally. No money crosses the border.
5️⃣
Ledger balanced digitallyInternational ledger entries record the obligations on both sides. Settlement is complete.

Who Are Agents?

👤 Agents are ordinary bank customers — not employees, not external companies. They have consented to participate in the mPOS network and have available liquidity. They earn a share of the fee on every transaction they facilitate. The engine assigns them randomly — the sender never knows who their agent is, and the agent never knows who the sender is.

Nigeria Agents 🇳🇬

💡 For sender agents, the engine checks their international ledger balance — not their savings account. The ledger is what facilitates the transaction on the sending side.

UK Agents 🇬🇧

💡 For recipient agents, the engine checks their local fiat liquidity — because they need actual GBP to pay the recipient locally.

What is the International Ledger?

💡 Think of the international ledger like a shared scorecard. When Nigeria sends to the UK, the scorecard records: Nigeria side owes less, UK side is owed more. No money physically moved. The obligation is balanced digitally. Agents settle the obligation locally on each side.
✅ The ledger is denominated in each agent's own local currency. It appears in their bank app as a standard account line — same symbol, same value. It is not a separate currency, not a token, not a reserve pool. It is simply an accounting record.

How Agent Conversion Works

💡 After facilitating a transaction, an agent holds an international ledger balance. They can convert this back to local fiat at any time — immediately if they want, or within 3 days maximum. They exchange with another agent in the same country. This keeps ledger value circulating actively across the network.

Why This Matters

⚖️ The conversion cycle is what keeps the entire network healthy. Agents who convert promptly are rewarded with a lower fee. Agents who delay are automatically corrected by the engine. Cross-border ledger exchange is blocked — value can only circulate domestically between same-country agents. This prevents hoarding and corridor imbalance.
