What is mPOS and how does it work?
mPOS is a global digital settlement layer that sits invisibly inside existing banks. It allows money to be sent across borders — near-instantly, at low cost — without physically moving cash across countries. This page lets you submit a live transfer to see the entire system in action.

Step 3 of 6 — The Agents
Who are agents and what do they do?
Agents are ordinary bank customers — not employees, not external companies. They have consented to participate in the mPOS network and hold available liquidity. They earn a share of the fee on every transaction they facilitate. The engine assigns them randomly — no one can choose their agent.
Nigeria Agents 🇳🇬 — Sending Side
What Nigeria agents do When a sender in Nigeria submits a transfer, the engine picks a Nigeria agent randomly. That agent receives the sender's NGN into their savings account. In return, the engine records a debit against their international ledger balance — the "IOU" that gets balanced on the other side.
UK Agents 🇬🇧 — Receiving Side
What UK agents do The engine picks a UK agent randomly. That agent pays GBP directly to the recipient from their current account. The engine then credits their international ledger balance — recording that they are owed that value back.
Why random assignment matters Neither the sender, the recipient, nor the agents can choose each other. The engine decides. This prevents collusion, preferential routing, and manipulation. It is a core compliance feature — not just a technical detail.

Step 4 of 6 — The Ledger
What is the International Ledger?
The international ledger is like a shared scorecard. When Nigeria sends to the UK, the scorecard records: Nigeria side owes less, UK side is owed more. No money physically moves. The obligation is balanced digitally. Agents settle the obligation locally on each side.

How the Ledger Works
It looks like a normal bank account line The ledger balance appears in each agent's bank app as a standard account — same currency symbol, same format. It is not a token, not a crypto asset, not a separate currency. It is simply an accounting record. The agent sees "International Account: £4,100" and that is all they need to know.

Recipient-Side Buffer (0.2%)
What is the buffer? Of the 1.2% total fee, 0.2% is retained on the recipient side in destination currency. For example, on a £100 transaction, £0.20 is held back. This accumulates across all corridors — creating a collective stabilisation reserve. Because corridors rarely all experience stress at the same time, this reserve is self-sustaining.

Step 5 of 6 — Conversion Cycle
How do agents convert their ledger balance back to cash?
After facilitating a transaction, an agent holds an international ledger balance. They must convert this back to local cash — either immediately (at a low fee) or within 3 days maximum. If they miss the window, the engine converts it automatically at a higher fee. This keeps the network healthy and liquid.
